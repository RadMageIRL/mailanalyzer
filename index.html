    Troubleshooting report

    
      
    Masking is partial, not anonymization. It hides the mailbox part of addresses (j***@example.com) and nothing else: display names, domains, host names, IP addresses, the subject (unless hidden) and encoded values such as tracking IDs stay as they are. Message-ID and Network-Message-Id are never masked; they are what Message trace and Threat Explorer search on. Read the report before you share it.

      MailAnalyzer_
      
      
      A zero-dependency, local-first email header analyzer. Headers are parsed by this page in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or looked up in DNS. This browser remembers the theme, the report options, your domains and the recent list (header blocks only; remove items or clear it from the Recent dialog). Outlook .msg files are read here too: MailAnalyzer takes out only the stored internet headers. Raw headers and message IDs can identify people and systems, so treat what you paste like the message itself.
      
      
      what MailAnalyzer does not do
      
      
      It does not verify signatures, query SPF/DMARC records, or check IP reputation. Every verdict comes from a header a mail server wrote, and is labeled reported. Headers that were already present when the message reached your systems could have been written by the sender, so MailAnalyzer flags verdict headers found there as untrusted. That trust boundary is inferred: it is the newest hop received by Exchange Online's inbound front door (*.mail.protection.outlook.com) or a host on your domains (below), and only when every newer hop is Microsoft 365 or yours. Without one, every verdict is shown as a claim. Microsoft's own X-MS-Exchange-* / X-Forefront-* stamps are judged by name instead, because Exchange appends them at the bottom and renames copies that arrive from outside to …-Untrusted.
      
      
      what it reads
      
      
      The Received chain, Authentication-Results / ARC / Received-SPF / DKIM-Signature, Microsoft 365 X-Forefront-Antispam-Report (SFV, SCL, CAT, IPV, SFTY, SRV, CIP, CTRY…), X-Microsoft-Antispam (BCL, PCL), composite authentication reason codes, X-MS-Exchange-Organization-* stamps, the Network-Message-Id and X-Microsoft-Antispam-Mailbox-Delivery, plus X-Mailgun-*, SpamAssassin / rspamd / virus scanner stamps (X-Spam-*, X-Spamd-Result, X-Rspamd-Action, X-Virus-Status), X-Proofpoint-*, X-Mimecast-*, and the list and automation headers (List-Unsubscribe, List-Id, Precedence, Auto-Submitted).
      
      
      categories
      
      
      The headline names the most serious category any finding supports: malware, phishing, impersonation, spam, bulk or automated. A verdict a filter wrote (Microsoft 365, SpamAssassin, rspamd, a virus scanner) makes it reported. Otherwise it is MailAnalyzer's own reading of the headers, marked inferred and worded likely or possible: lookalike and international domains, brand names in the sender name, free webmail used for business roles or replies, repeated headers and hidden characters. Lookalikes are checked against your domains and a short list of commonly impersonated brands, with no lookups.
      
      
      your domains
      
      
      
      Your organization's mail domains and server names, separated by commas. A host on one of them, or on a subdomain, counts as your own server when MailAnalyzer looks for where a message entered your systems; Exchange Online is recognized without this. * matches any characters, for server names like mail*. Saved in this browser only.
